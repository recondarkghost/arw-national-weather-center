Weather Field Guide

How forecasters read the atmosphere

01

Radar Reflectivity

Reflectivity shows energy returned to the radar. Stronger colors can indicate heavier precipitation, but hail and non-weather objects can also produce stronger returns.

02

Radar Velocity

Velocity measures motion toward or away from the radar. Strong inbound and outbound differences near each other can indicate rotation, but trained interpretation is essential.

03

Satellite Imagery

Visible satellite imagery shows reflected daylight. Infrared imagery measures cloud-top temperatures and continues operating at night.

04

Outlooks and Warnings

Outlooks describe probability across an area. A watch means prepare for possible hazardous weather. A warning means dangerous weather is occurring or imminent and people should act.

American Recon Weather Unit Field Guide

Learn to Read Weather Like a Forecaster

These illustrated guides explain what the colors, lines and patterns mean. Radar interpretation supports situational awareness, but official National Weather Service warnings must always guide protective action.

01 RADAR

Radar Reflectivity

Reflectivity estimates the amount of energy returned by rain, snow, hail or other objects.

Read it: stronger colors normally mean stronger returns, not automatically a tornado.
02 RADAR

Radar Velocity

Velocity measures movement toward or away from one radar station. Green commonly represents inbound motion and red outbound motion.

Read it: tightly packed opposite colors can suggest rotation, but trained interpretation is required.
03 ATMOSPHERE

How Clouds Form

Air rises, expands and cools. When it reaches saturation, water vapor condenses onto tiny particles and forms cloud droplets.

Read it: growing vertical clouds can indicate increasing instability and stronger upward motion.
04 SATELLITE

Visible vs. Infrared

Visible imagery uses reflected daylight. Infrared imagery measures cloud-top temperature and works during both day and night.

Read it: very cold cloud tops can indicate high clouds and powerful thunderstorm updrafts.
05 TEMPERATURE

Temperature vs. Heat Index

Temperature measures the air. Heat index estimates how hot it feels when humidity reduces the body's ability to cool by sweating.

Read it: heat index is not the measured air temperature; direct sun can make conditions feel hotter.
06 WEATHER MAPS

Fronts and Pressure

Cold and warm fronts mark boundaries between air masses. Low pressure commonly supports rising air, clouds and unsettled weather.

Read it: fronts show boundaries and movement, not an exact wall where weather instantly changes.
07 SEVERE WEATHER

Possible Rotation Signature

A velocity couplet is a tight area of winds moving toward and away from the radar next to each other.

Read it: a couplet can support a warning decision but does not by itself prove that a tornado is on the ground.
08 ALERTS

Outlook, Watch and Warning

An outlook highlights possible risk. A watch means hazardous weather is possible. A warning means danger is occurring or imminent.

Read it: warning information and local instructions take priority over every map or social-media report.
09 FORECASTS

Rainfall Forecast Maps

Rainfall maps estimate total precipitation during a stated period. Colors represent forecast ranges shown by the product legend.

Read it: always check whether a map covers 24 hours, several days or an excessive-rainfall probability.
